DAY7
Day 7 · October 24, 2026

the coffin, by hand

Draft: not published yet

This one is a long night. My first message went out at ten past midnight and the last before a break at 5:52 a.m. In between I worked on three things at once: the music, the pause menu, and the coffin in the save room.

Music without gaps

At 12:28 a.m. I'd been listening to Sega CD soundtracks and noticed some games play audio files through the Mega CD's sound chip. Could that give us the original soundtrack without the music stopping between rooms? Loads are short, two seconds at most. A two-second buffer might be enough.

I was specific about what I didn't want: silence is better than a song that jumps to a different part. When the drive is done loading, the music has to continue exactly where the buffer ended. Then, while we talked it through, I worked out the catch myself. If the sound chip is busy streaming the music, who plays the sound effects?

At 12:57 a.m. I said it out loud: the best way out is still rewriting the music for the Mega Drive's FM chip and keeping the Mega CD's sample chip for all the effects. We tested the stream anyway. At 3:46 a.m., after seeing how few channels were left for effects in that mode, I wrote: "Are we really that limited for sound effects?? That's a huge problem." (Translated from Portuguese.)

The coffin, again

By 1 a.m. the 3D coffin had a crystal and a cloud of doritos that looked right. At 1:04 a.m. I remembered how the original actually did it: the faces of the crystal flew outward along the depth axis and dissolved on the way. At 3:53 the crystal and the doritos were great, but the coffin still scrambled while turning, especially when seen from the side.

At 2:56 a.m. I asked the question that changed direction: would it be worth making the coffin out of sprites too? Then, a few minutes apart: make it appear and disappear with the same dither as the doors, stop it from breaking apart on the way in and out, and remove the lines on its edges. At 3:41 a.m., after looking at the original again, I gave up on it entirely: "No, the original save coffin is really ugly. Let's go with pixel art." (Translated from Portuguese.)

Pixel art, three ways

Claude drew a first coffin that opens and closes. I said it had come out too different from the model, with too few details. The second was much better, but the inside was flat: it needed buttoned velvet, with more shading so it looks padded.

At 4 a.m. I made one myself that I thought looked good. I also tried Gemini. My first attempt, a video of Alucard lying down in the coffin, modernized everything. Later it drew the first poses of him lying down well, and I asked Claude to fix the proportions of three where Alucard grew taller with each frame, using one of his own sprites, laid down, as the reference.

Then I described the whole sequence. Alucard walks in. Press up and the coffin opens, with the save menu. Choose to save and he jumps; at the top of the jump he changes pose and floats down into the coffin, which closes.

At 10:59 a.m., back at it, I saw the problem: the coffin was drawn in an isometric view that doesn't match the room's perspective. From where the camera is, you should only see its side. Claude redrew it from the side, reusing the lid animation.

At 11:32 a.m. I changed the effect of the descent. Instead of after-images, the same treatment as the relics: a blinking white outline on Alucard and white particles floating from his body until the lid closes. Twenty minutes later I sent Claude a sheet I had put together myself showing exactly how I wanted it: Alucard coming down in slow motion, as if floating, switching to the next pose at certain heights, with the outline disappearing on the third pose.

One more rule at 12:29 p.m.: choosing not to save still restores HP and MP.

When the coffin went into the game, it had too few colors; the conversion didn't translate it well. I asked for options that used the save room's palette and the colors normally reserved for enemies, since no enemy will ever appear there. At 2 p.m. I picked the fifth option.

The menu I drew

At 2:26 a.m., in another chat, I asked for the pause menu to open with Start during gameplay, using a design I attached, but keeping the original game's fonts. At 12:30 p.m. I asked to reuse the menu's dialog boxes for the item and enemy name notices, instead of the plain white box from two days earlier. The pause menu deserves its own post, and will get one.

Black trees

At 12:33 p.m. I found a problem in the opening: the trees in the forest had a black background that covered the scenery behind them. Claude suggested options. At 1:44 p.m. I settled it: just remove the background behind the trees and leave it black.

The first big merge

At 2:04 p.m. I asked every chat to put its finished work in a folder called "build final". At 2:22 p.m. I opened a new chat with one job: find those folders and add everything to the main build. Between 2:36 and 2:39 p.m. it did.

The number

54 commits, 82 messages from me. One coffin, drawn three times.

Tomorrow: Sunday, and fewer messages.

Howthis is made

I decide what the port should be, draw and pick colors, and test every build. The code, tools and measurements are written by Claude (Anthropic's AI). This post was drafted by Claude from my chat messages and the project's commit history, and reviewed by me. The coffin images are previews Claude rendered during that work.
